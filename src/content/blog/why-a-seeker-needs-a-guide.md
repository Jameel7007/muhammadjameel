There is a modern conviction, flattering and false, that a sincere person with good books needs nothing else. I believed it for years. I had the books. I underlined them. And I circled the same three insights for a decade, the way a man lost in the woods circles the same three trees.

No one climbs an unknown mountain without someone who has been to the summit. Not because the climber is weak, but because the mountain is honest: it does not rearrange itself to spare your feelings. The self does. The self is the one terrain that lies to its own surveyor, and this is why the tradition insists on a guide.[^1]

> "A book will tell you the truth. Only a teacher will tell you the truth about you."

When I sat with Shaykh Hisham, what changed was not my information. It was my exposure. A guide is a mirror that cannot be negotiated with, and the companionship of such a mirror, what the tradition calls suhba, accomplishes in a season what solitary reading could not accomplish in a lifetime.

This is not a call to abandon discernment. The tradition is severe about the qualifications of a true guide precisely because the counterfeit is common. But the answer to false teachers has never been no teachers, any more than the answer to bad medicine is no physicians. Find the one with a chain of transmission, a character that survives proximity, and no appetite for your money or your awe. Then stay.

[^1]: "Whoever has no shaykh, his shaykh is Shaytan" is the classical formulation, attributed to Bayazid al-Bistami among others.
