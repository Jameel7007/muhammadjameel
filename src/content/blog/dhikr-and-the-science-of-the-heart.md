People new to the path often mistake dhikr for a mood: a warm feeling produced by candlelight and repetition. The masters spoke of it as a method, with the sobriety of physicians. The heart, they taught, rusts the way iron rusts, and remembrance is its polish.[^1]

A method implies dosage, regularity, and supervision. This is why the orders prescribe a daily portion, the wird, rather than leaving remembrance to inspiration. No one becomes strong by lifting weights only on the days they feel moved to. The heart is trained the same way the body is: by showing up when nothing in you wants to.

> "The tongue repeats until the heart takes over. Then the heart repeats until it is taken over."

The Naqshbandi masters describe stations of remembrance: of the tongue, of the heart, of the secret. Each is quieter than the last. The beginner counts beads; the advanced seeker discovers that something in the chest has begun counting without them. What started as an act becomes a state, and what was a state becomes a station.

I have come to think the Honorable Elijah Muhammad was pointing at the same science when he told his students that the hardest battle is with the self, and that the self is remade by what it repeats. Repetition is the technology. The question is only what we are repeating, and in whose name.

[^1]: The image of the heart's rust and its polish is drawn from a well-known tradition of the Prophet, elaborated at length in the manuals of the path.
